How Do I GetMore Customers?

Most businesses don’t need more leads first. They need to stop losing the ones already calling.

To get more customers, answer every call, form and message within minutes, text back every call you miss, follow up until each lead books or says no, and put every lead in one inbox. Only then spend more on your website and on ads.

The Short Answer

More leads won’t help if the ones you already get are slipping away.

Fast replies belong first, ahead of ads and a new website, because every extra lead you pay for goes through the same leaky process. Fix these five things in this order.

In This Order

  1. Count Your LeakCalls, forms and messages for one month, next to how many booked.
  2. Answer Every Lead FastIncluding the calls you miss, with a text back in seconds.
  3. Follow Up Until They Book Or Say NoMany leads are lost to no follow up, not a slow first reply.
  4. Put Every Lead In One InboxCalls, texts, forms and every app, all in one place.
  5. Then Fix The Website, Then Get More TrafficSpeed first, then Quality, then Volume.

I. Count Your Leak

Pick last month. Add up every call, web form and message from every source: your phone, your website, Google, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp, Thumbtack and anything else. Then count how many of those people actually booked.

Your phone’s call log shows missed calls. Your email shows forms. Each app shows its own messages. Your calendar or invoices show who booked. Put it side by side, and you can see where people drop off in your own numbers.

Example numbers, not real data

45Booked

20Missed calls never returned

30Got one reply, then nothing

25Said no or hired someone else

In this example, 120 people reached out and 45 booked. The 50 who were missed or never followed up are the leak. Win back one in five of them and that would be 10 more customers without one more ad.

II. Answer Every Lead Fast

Including the calls you miss.

When someone needs help now, they may contact a few businesses at once, and the first one to get back to them gets the first shot. So the goal is minutes, not hours. Harvard Business Review research on online sales leads found most companies are not responding nearly fast enough.

  • Every missed call gets a text back within seconds, so the caller knows you saw it and can book a time.
  • Every web form gets a reply within a few minutes, day or night.
  • Messages from Google, Facebook, Instagram and lead apps get answered in the same few minutes.
  • Every first reply gives them an easy next step, like a time to talk or a link to book.

III. Follow Up Until They Book Or Say No

This is where a lot of leads are lost.

A fast first reply catches the people who were ready to book today. Everyone else needs a few more messages, and many businesses stop after one. People get busy, forget, or wait for payday. A short, friendly follow up a few days later can be what turns them into a customer.

Here’s a simple rhythm you can use in almost any trade.

Same DayText
Next DayCall
Day 3Text
Day 7Email
Day 14Text
MonthlyCheck In

Stop the moment they book or ask you to stop. Only text people who reached out to you or agreed to hear from you, and always include a way to opt out. Give past customers the same care, because a short check in can bring some of them back.

IV. Put Every Lead In One Inbox

Leads come in from your phone, your website, Google, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp, Thumbtack, Angi and more. When each one lives in its own app, some wait for days because nobody opened that app. Bring every source into one inbox, or into the CRM you already use, so every lead gets the same fast reply and the same follow up.

V. Then Fix The Website, Then Get More Traffic

Once every lead is answered and followed up, a better website is the next fix, so more of the people who find you pick you. After that, more traffic from ads, Google and AI search has a real chance to turn into customers instead of missed calls.

Speed: Get Booked. Quality: Get Picked. Volume: Get Found.

Most agencies sell volume first. That’s backwards. We start with speed.

In Your Trade

The five steps are the same for any trade in any city. Here’s where the leak can hide in a few of them.

Roofing

Storm weeks can bring a flood of calls while every crew is up on a roof. The leak is the call nobody picks up and the estimate nobody chases. Text back every missed call, and follow up on every estimate until the homeowner signs or says no.

Law Firms

People often call a lawyer right after something goes wrong, and that can be at night or on a weekend. If nobody answers, they may call the next firm. The leak is intake that waits until morning. Answer or text back right away, book the consultation in that first reply, and follow up until they decide.

HVAC

When the AC dies in July, a homeowner may call a few companies, and the first one to get back to them gets the first shot. The leak is the missed call during a busy day and the replacement quote nobody follows up on. Text back in seconds, and keep following up on every big quote.

Med Spas

New clients may reach out after hours, from Instagram, a web form or a missed call. The leak is the consultation request that sits until the next day. Reply fast to book the consultation, keep follow ups about scheduling only, and never ask for or repeat health details in a text or email.

Restaurants

The calls that matter most are catering orders, large parties and private events, and they can come in during the dinner rush when nobody can answer. The leak is the event inquiry that never gets a callback. Text back every missed call and follow up on every event request until it’s booked.

Auto Detailing

Detailers often have their hands full when the phone rings, and people may take a few days to decide on a ceramic coating or paint protection quote. The leak is the missed call and the quote that never gets a second message. Text back while you work, and follow up until they book a date.

Questions, Answered

I

Why am I getting calls but not customers?

Often because the leads slip away after the first contact. Calls go to voicemail and never get returned, forms get one reply and nothing after, and messages sit in an app nobody checks. Count your calls, forms and messages for one month next to how many booked, and the gap shows you where they went.

II

How do I stop losing leads?

Answer every lead within minutes, text back every call you miss right away, follow up by text, call and email until the lead books or says no, and bring every lead source into one inbox so nothing waits where nobody looks.

III

Is it bad to miss calls from customers?

Missing a call is normal when you’re busy with a customer. Leaving it unanswered is what costs you, because the caller may just try the next business on the list. Send a text back within seconds and call back as soon as you can, and a missed call can still turn into a booking.

IV

How fast should I respond to a new lead?

Within minutes, not hours. Harvard Business Review research on online sales leads found most companies are not responding nearly fast enough. Start with a text back the moment you miss a call and a reply to every form within a few minutes.

V

How many times should I follow up with a lead?

Keep going until they book or tell you no. A simple rhythm is the same day, the next day, day three, day seven and day fourteen, then a light check in once a month, mixing texts, calls and emails, and stopping the moment someone asks you to.

VI

Should I fix my website or buy more ads first?

Neither. Fix how fast you answer and follow up first, because more traffic into a business that misses calls just loses more customers. Then improve the website so more visitors pick you, and only then buy or earn more traffic. Speed first, then Quality, then Volume.

Sources

Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, “The Short Life of Online Sales Leads,” Harvard Business Review, March 2011. The example month above uses made up numbers to show how to count, not real client data.
