Tattoo Artist - In Progress

Dylan Prorok

A tattoo artist concept built around scale, body flow, and the choice to begin a long project.

A large tattoo has to read from across the room.

The design problem

Show the scale before asking for the inquiry.

Large-scale tattooing is not a quick product choice. The work has to fit a body, remain legible, and make sense across a series of sessions. The concept gives those realities visual weight before presenting the decision to begin.

The ink transition moves from atmosphere into the artist at work. Portrait, body, and negative space stay large enough to communicate the commitment instead of collapsing into a gallery of small tiles.

The information sequence

Answer what a serious project asks.

Scale

Open with work at a size that lets composition, line weight, and distance read clearly.

Body flow

Explain that the piece is designed for the person and placement it will live on, not dropped onto the body as a flat image.

Commitment

Prepare the visitor for a conversation and an ongoing project before the inquiry action appears.
